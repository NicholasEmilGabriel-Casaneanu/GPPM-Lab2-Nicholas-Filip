Mathematics

Mandelbrot Set

z_{n+1} = z_n² + c. For each point c in the plane, ask: does this stay bounded as we iterate? The answer paints one of mathematics' most famous fractals. Click anywhere to zoom in — there's infinite detail at every scale.

Mathematics

Prime Sieve (Sieve of Eratosthenes)

Watch Eratosthenes' ancient algorithm in action: start with a grid of numbers and strike out multiples of each prime as it's found. Adjust the range and step through the sieve to see which numbers survive — and why.

Mathematics

Collatz Conjecture

Pick any positive integer. If it's even, halve it; if it's odd, triple it and add one. Every number tested so far eventually reaches 1 — but nobody has proven it always will. Plot the trajectory and watch the famous "hailstone" spikes.

Mathematics

Logistic Map & Chaos

x_{n+1} = r·x_n·(1 − x_n). One tiny equation, an entire zoo of behaviour: fixed points, cycles, and the onset of chaos as r increases. Drag r through the bifurcation diagram and see order collapse into unpredictability.

Fourier Series

Any repeating shape can be built from sine waves. Add terms one by one and watch a square wave, sawtooth, or triangle emerge from spinning circles. Drag the number of harmonics to see where the Gibbs overshoot refuses to disappear.

Mathematics

Pascal's Triangle & Fractals

Color the entries of Pascal's triangle by divisibility — say, by whether each number is even or divisible by 3 — and Sierpiński's triangle appears out of thin air. Switch the modulus and watch a different fractal emerge.

Mathematics

Monty Hall Problem

Three doors, one prize. Pick one, the host opens a losing door, and you may switch. Run thousands of trials with "always stay" vs "always switch" and watch the win rates settle at 1/3 and 2/3 — the probability your intuition argues with.

Mathematics

Buffon's Needle

Drop needles onto a lined floor and count how often they cross a line. The fraction converges on 2/π — a way to estimate π by pure chance. Increase the number of drops and watch the running estimate wiggle toward the true value.

Beat Frequencies

Two close pure tones — when added, they produce a slow amplitude beat at the difference frequency. Adjust the two pitches and watch the envelope wow in and out. Click Play to also hear it: musicians use this exact phenomenon to tune by ear.

Carbon Cycle

Carbon flows between four reservoirs — atmosphere, surface ocean, deep ocean, and land biosphere — driven by photosynthesis, respiration, ocean exchange, and human emissions. Drag the emission rate and watch each pool evolve over a century. The atmospheric pool is what drives global temperature.

Psychology

Stroop Test

Color words printed in mismatched ink — name the INK color, not the word. The classic Stroop effect: when word and color disagree, your brain takes longer to suppress reading. Run timed trials and watch your reaction time double on incongruent stimuli.
