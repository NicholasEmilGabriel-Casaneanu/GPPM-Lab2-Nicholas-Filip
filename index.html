Music & Acoustics

Beat Frequencies

Two close pure tones — when added, they produce a slow amplitude beat at the difference frequency. Adjust the two pitches and watch the envelope wow in and out. Click Play to also hear it: musicians use this exact phenomenon to tune by ear.

Open sim
Music & Acoustics

Harmonic Series

Every pitched note is really a stack of harmonics — integer multiples of a fundamental. Toggle individual harmonics on and off and hear how a pure tone becomes a trumpet, a violin, or a clarinet depending on which overtones dominate.

Open sim
Music & Acoustics

Standing Waves

A guitar string fixed at both ends only vibrates at certain frequencies — the harmonics. Change tension, length, and harmonic number and watch the nodes and antinodes move. This is why strings sound the way they do.

Open sim
Music & Acoustics

Equal Temperament

Why is a piano slightly out of tune with itself? Compare just intonation (pure ratios like 3:2) with equal temperament (twelfth root of 2). The small errors are what let you play in every key.

Open sim
Music & Acoustics

Fourier Synthesis of Instruments

Any periodic waveform — the sound of a violin, oboe, or synth — is a sum of sine waves. Add harmonics with different amplitudes and hear the timbre shift from pure tone to something that sounds like a real instrument.

Open sim
Music & Acoustics

Doppler Effect

An ambulance siren sounds higher as it approaches and lower as it recedes. Move a source past a listener and watch the sound waves bunch up in front and stretch behind — the same effect that lets police radar measure speed.

Climate & Sustainability 🧪 Lab

Carbon Cycle

Carbon flows between four reservoirs — atmosphere, surface ocean, deep ocean, and land biosphere — driven by photosynthesis, respiration, ocean exchange, and human emissions. Drag the emission rate and watch each pool evolve over a century. The atmospheric pool is what drives global temperature.

Climate & Sustainability

Greenhouse Effect

Sunlight comes in; infrared goes out. Greenhouse gases trap some of that outgoing heat. Change CO₂ concentration and see how the planet's energy balance shifts — and why a few hundred parts per million matters so much.

Climate & Sustainability

Ice-Albedo Feedback

White ice reflects sunlight; dark ocean absorbs it. Warm the planet a little and ice melts, which makes the surface darker, which makes it warm faster. See the runaway feedback loop in action.

Climate & Sustainability

Sea Level Rise

Two processes drive sea level: water expands as it warms, and glaciers add meltwater. Toggle each contribution and watch how a coastline changes over the next century. The difference between 1 °C and 3 °C of warming is enormous.

Energy Mix

Build a power grid: choose how much comes from coal, gas, nuclear, solar, wind, and hydro. Each source has a different carbon cost and price. Try to hit a 2035 emissions target without breaking the bank.

Climate & Sustainability

Keeling Curve

Since 1958, CO₂ has been measured continuously at Mauna Loa, Hawaii. The famous "sawtooth" curve climbs relentlessly but wiggles every year as the Northern Hemisphere breathes in and out. See the data and the seasonality.

Psychology

Stroop Test

Color words printed in mismatched ink — name the INK color, not the word. The classic Stroop effect: when word and color disagree, your brain takes longer to suppress reading. Run timed trials and watch your reaction time double on incongruent stimuli.

Psychology

Reaction Time

Click as soon as the screen turns green. Measure your simple reaction time over dozens of trials and see how it compares to the human average (~250 ms). Watch for the outlier trials where you anticipated too early.

Psychology

Memory Span

Digits flash one at a time. Repeat them back in order (forward span) or reversed (backward span). Most adults hold 7 ± 2 digits forward and 5 ± 1 backward. Find your own limit.

Cognitive Science

Change Blindness

Two scenes flicker back and forth. One small thing changes — can you spot it? Most people are surprisingly bad at this, because attention is narrower than it feels. Find the change, then see how obvious it was all along.

Cognitive Science

Mental Rotation

Two 3D shapes appear side by side. Are they the same object seen from a different angle, or a mirror-image impostor? Shepard & Metzler found that reaction time rises linearly with rotation angle — your brain really is spinning the shape.

Cognitive Science

Dual N-Back

The famous working-memory training task: track both a shape and a sound, and press "match" when either repeats from N steps ago. N=2 is easy. N=3 is hard. It's the cognitive equivalent of a treadmill.

Cognitive Science

Attentional Blink

Letters flash in rapid succession. Spot the white target letter, then try to spot a second target shortly after. In the ~300–500 ms window after the first, the second is often missed entirely — attention is still "blinking."

Cognitive Science

McGurk Effect

Hear one syllable, see another mouth shape — and your brain fuses them into a third sound that was never played. Switch audio and video independently and discover that you don't hear with your ears alone.
