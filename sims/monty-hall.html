Why is switching better?

Your first pick wins 1/3 of the time. That means the other two doors together win 2/3. When the host opens a losing door, that 2/3 probability collapses onto the remaining door — so switching wins 2/3 of the time.

It feels wrong because the host's action looks like a random reveal, but it isn't: he always opens a losing door, and he knows where the prize is. That information advantage is what changes the odds. Run 100,000 trials and watch the bars lock to 33% vs 67%.
