Why this works

If a number has a factor larger than its square root, it must also have a matching factor smaller than its square root. So we only need to sieve with primes up to √n — everything else is already taken care of.

Notice that when we start crossing off multiples of p, we begin at p², not 2p. Smaller multiples were already removed by a smaller prime. This is why the sieve is so fast: complexity is O(n log log n).
