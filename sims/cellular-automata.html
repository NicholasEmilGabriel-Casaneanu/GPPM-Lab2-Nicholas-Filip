Why is Rule 110 special?

Rule 110 is Turing-complete — it can, in principle, compute anything any computer can. A strip of black and white cells following a one-line rule can run programs. Stephen Wolfram conjectured this in 1985; Matthew Cook proved it in 2004.

Rule 30, on the other hand, is chaotic: it's used as a pseudorandom number generator and even used to be the random source in Mathematica. Rule 90 makes the Sierpiński triangle we saw earlier.
