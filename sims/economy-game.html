How this works

Bills come due at the start of each year. Your salary for the year is deposited first, then bills are subtracted. You only need enough cash on hand to cover the gap — but if you're short, you take an emergency loan at 20% APR and unpaid balances compound.

Cash decays. Money sitting in your wallet loses value to inflation every year. The bank pays interest (inflation + 0.5–3%), so anything not invested or saved actively loses you money.

Possessions cost money. A house needs property tax and maintenance. A car needs insurance, fuel, and occasional repairs. Miss a payment and you'll lose the asset — or worse.

Emergencies draw from savings. Medical bills, car repairs, and home emergencies will pull from your cash first, then your savings, and only borrow as a last resort. Save actively — it's your emergency fund.

The casino is real. Slots, roulette, and Jacks-or-Better video poker. You can win big — but the house edge is real and compounded by psychology. Gambling increases your hidden "risk-taker" trait, which makes your stock market returns more volatile. Play too long and you'll be revealed at retirement as a compulsive gambler.

Bankruptcy is real. If your net worth drops below −$5,000, the game ends. No restart from within.
