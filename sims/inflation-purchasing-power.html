The Rule of 70

Dividing 70 by the inflation rate gives the doubling time of prices. At 2% inflation, prices double every 35 years. At 7%, every 10 years. At 20%, every 3.5 years. That's why central banks target ~2% — low enough to be invisible, high enough to avoid deflation.

Notice that the cost curve is exponential: money doesn't lose value by the same dollar amount each year, it loses value by the same percentage. That's why inflation feels so different at 2% than at 8% — one is nearly invisible, the other erodes savings fast.
