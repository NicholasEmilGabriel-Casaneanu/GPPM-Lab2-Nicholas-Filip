How this estimates π

If a needle of length L is dropped on a floor with lines spaced d apart, the probability it crosses a line is P = 2L / (πd). Solve for π: π = 2L / (Pd). So the more drops you do, the more accurately you can estimate π — from pure randomness!

But convergence is slow: the error shrinks like 1/√n. To halve the error, you need 4× more drops. That's why 100,000 needles only gets you π to about 2 decimal places. This is the price of Monte Carlo methods.
